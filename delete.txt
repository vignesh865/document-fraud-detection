Subject: Experimental Document Fraud Detection Strategies

Hi Tom,

I’ve been spending some of my free time exploring new approaches to enhance our existing document fraud detection work. I wanted to share a high-level overview of six experimental strategies I’ve mapped out, ranging from simple metadata checks to advanced AI-driven forensics.

Here is a quick summary of the approaches:

Gemini AI Direct Forensic Analysis: Leveraging the Gemini Vision model as a "forensics expert" to holistically validate logical consistency (e.g., age vs. birthdate), visual artifacts, and document structure.
Traditional Computer Vision: Applying classical, non-AI techniques (like Error Level Analysis and noise variance) to detect editing artifacts without external dependencies.
Region-Aware Analysis: Using AI to segment the document into semantic parts (Photo, Text, Seal) so we can target specific computer vision tools to the regions where they are most effective.
Region-Based Frequency Detection: analyzing the invisible frequency domain of specific regions to identify "deepfake" or AI-generated content that looks perfect to the naked eye.
Embedding-Based Region Consistency: treating the document as a puzzle and comparing the "visual style" (embeddings) of different regions to detect if a photo or text block was pasted from a different source.
Hybrid Multi-Tier System: A cost-efficient workflow that filters documents through layers, starting with cheap metadata/logic checks and only escalating ambiguous cases to deeper forensic analysis.
I’ve documented the methodology for each of these. Let me know if you’d like to walk through any of them in more detail to see if they could fit into our roadmap.
